# Acceptance Criteria & Impact Results

## Acceptance Criteria

**Device**
- Meets residential building code
- Easy to operate
- Training resources available to operate efficiently

**Implementation**
- Programmed to run correctly at all times
- Able to integrate with existing smart home devices
- Sends notifications for maintenance and minimizes downtime

**Warning System**
- Tested and functional during real-world scenarios
- Reliable, with an appropriate response time
- Accurate, and disengages once the appropriate action is taken

## Standards Referenced
- **IEEE 1621-2004** — User interface elements in power control of consumer electronics
- **IEEE 1680-2009** — Environmental assessment of electronic products
- **IEEE 802.11** — WLAN telecommunications standard (Wi-Fi module compliance)
- **NFPA 72** — National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code
- **NFPA 70** — National Electrical Code (wiring, circuit protection, power supply)
- **ADA 702.1** — Fire alarm systems must have permanently installed audible and visible alarms

## Impact Summary

**Safety**
- 2022 national fire estimate: 374,000 fires, 2,720 deaths, 10,250 injuries
- 2013–2022 trend: 4% decrease in fires, but a 1% *increase* in deaths — reinforcing the need for earlier detection, not just fewer incidents
- System impact: earlier detection → lower emergency response costs → increased occupant peace of mind

**Environmental**
- Natural gas accounts for roughly 35% of total U.S. energy-related CO2 emissions (EIA) — early leak detection helps reduce unnecessary release
- Reducing indoor humidity swings (target: 30–50%, max 60%) lowers mold/allergen risk and associated health interventions

**Economic** (U.S. residential gas-related fires, annual)
- Property damage: **$10B+**
- Medical costs: **$5B+**
- Lost productivity: **$4B+**
- Death & injury costs: up to **$26B**

**Technical impact areas** (self-assessed 0–10 scale): real-time monitoring, automatic shutoff, advanced data analytics, remote access & control, smart-home integration, multi-sensor capability, reduced false alarms, energy efficiency, self-diagnostics.

**Global & social context**
- ~300,000 deaths from residential fires globally every year
- 90% of residential-fire deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries
- 83% of all fires reported in England in 2021 were residential
- 374,300 gas-leak-related residential fires in the U.S. in 2022

## Known Limitations (honest scope)
- Cannot currently alert first responders directly
- Does not yet include a home-security feature set
- Market appeal is currently U.S.-residential-focused rather than global
