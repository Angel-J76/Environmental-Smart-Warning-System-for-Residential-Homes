# Market & Engineering Requirements Analysis

## Problem Statement

Indoor gas leaks can evade scent detection, heightening the risks posed by associated methane emissions and indoor air pollution. U.S. fire departments spent roughly $500 million annually responding to gas leak incidents between 2003 and 2018, rising to $564 million by 2018 — the majority of these incidents involved no actual fire. Existing consumer systems fall short, underscoring the need for a proactive, sensor-based safety system.

## Target Audience
- Homeowners
- Property management and insurance services
- Emergency responders

## Marketing → Engineering Requirements

| Marketing Requirement | Engineering Requirement | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| User-friendly interface | Intuitive system for control and monitoring | Users need an easy-to-use interface for real-time monitoring |
| Mobile app integration | Support for wireless protocols (Wi-Fi) | Remote access enables users to monitor their home from anywhere and respond immediately to alerts |
| Quick installation | Plug-and-play hardware, easy setup | Homeowners expect a hassle-free installation process |
| Reliability and accuracy | Sensor accuracy (±0.1% gas, ±1% temp/humidity) | Reliable sensors reduce false alarms and build user trust |
| Multi-sensor functionality | Integration of multiple sensors (gas, smoke, temperature, humidity) | Covers a wider range of potential hazards |
| Compatibility with smart home devices | Support for communication with home automation platforms | Enables integration with platforms like Alexa or Google Home |
| Affordable pricing | Cost-effective components without compromising quality | Price-sensitive markets require affordability without sacrificing reliability |

## Competitive Comparison

| Product | Price | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **This System** | $133 | Detects harmful gases; easy to use; integrates with other home systems | Cannot alert first responders; limited global market appeal; no home security features |
| Shelly Gas LPG | $53 | Sound + light alarm with app notification; flame-resistant shell; compatible with Android, iOS, Alexa, home automation | Bulky design; continuous low stock; power-outlet only; hard to set up |
| X-Sense SC07 | $40 | 85 dB speaker; 10-year battery life; mobile app integration; high sensitivity | No smart home linking; cannot test from the app |
| First Alert SMI110 | $25 | Early warning tech; longevity; voice alerts | High sensitivity (more false positives); poor battery life |
| Smart Gas Leak Detector | $30 | User-friendly interface; smart home integration; voice alerts | Wi-Fi dependent reliability; limited battery life |

**Takeaway:** This system is priced higher than most single-purpose competitors, but is the only one in this comparison offering multi-sensor coverage (gas + temperature + humidity) combined with smart-home integration in one device.

## Project Deliverables

**Must be able to:**
- Detect gas leaks
- Monitor humidity levels
- Detect high temperatures

**System will be able to:**
- Connect to Wi-Fi
- Integrate with existing smart home systems

**Device will:**
- Operate in real time
- Have audible volume sufficient to alert occupants when threats are detected
